## 1. What the rulebook actually asks for

§16 is short and mechanical. A campaign is the same squad played through the missions, with four
things happening between them:

| § | Step | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 16/1 | Campaign procedure | One squad plays every mission; killed soldiers are replaced between missions; deeds are noted per soldier as they happen |
| 16/2 | Award Combat Points | Squad total = survivors × 4 if the mission was won, × 2 if lost. Every survivor gets 1 CP automatically; the rest are distributed **by the player**, at most 6 per soldier per mission. A recommended table gives the going rate for each deed (kill by fire 2, kill by assault 3, capture 3, knock out a tank 4, …) |
| 16/3 | Improve ratings | 6 CP buys +1 PC or +1 WS, or +2 DS, or an Initiative attempt: roll a die on the Initiative Increase Table, which gives IN or, failing, PC instead. Caps: IN 5, PC 9, WS +2, DS 8. Unspent CP carry over; a soldier under 6 CP cannot spend |
| 16/4 | Replacements | A dead soldier's Squad Points come back to the pool and are respent (they may be pooled across several dead soldiers); new soldiers roll PC/WS/DS and MPA normally; the whole squad is re-armed from the squad's Weapon Point total each mission |

Two rules matter more than they look:

- **An incapacitated soldier who was saved (exited, or carried to a building under Mission 1's
  ending) survives, earns CP, and starts the next mission unwounded.** Recovery is that sentence;
  there is no lingering-wounds system to model.
- **CP distribution is a player judgement, not a formula.** The rulebook's own worked example
  spends discretionary points on "he panicked at a critical moment" and "blowing the bridge was
  decisive". Any digital version either asks the player to allocate, or invents a policy the
  rulebook deliberately left open.

## 2. What we already have, and what is missing

**Already true, and cheap to build on:**

- Soldiers carry `IN / PC / WS / mpa / inventory / status` and are generated by
  `squadgenrules.js` from the real Squad Generation tables, with Squad Points and Weapon Points
  already modelled.
- `vpState.usCasualties` records every casualty with its kind (killed / incapacitated / captured),
  which is exactly the input §16/2 needs for "who survived".
- Save/load already serialises the whole game state (`serializeState`, version 1), so a
  between-missions save format is an extension of something tested, not a new subsystem.
- The victory target machinery (`victoryrules.js`) already scales a mission's requirement to the
  VP its paragraph data can actually yield, per mission.

**Missing for a campaign, in rough order of size:**

1. **A squad that outlives a mission.** Today the squad is generated at the start of Mission 1 and
   dies with the page. A campaign needs a persisted squad record: ratings, CP balance, Squad Point
   cost per soldier, deeds, and the squad's Weapon Point total.
2. **A between-missions screen**: award and distribute CP, spend CP on ratings (with the Initiative
   table's die roll — the one campaign rule that needs a visible die), buy replacements, re-arm.
3. **Deed tracking.** §16/2's award table is per deed (kill by fire, capture, knock out a tank).
   The engine already resolves every one of those events; nothing currently records who did what.
   This is the one piece of campaign work that touches the mission code.
4. **More missions.** See below — this is the real cost.

## 3. The prerequisite nobody has priced: missions 2–8

A campaign of one mission is a persistence shell. What each further mission needs:

| Mission | Map | Data present? | Blocked on |

| 6 Pleasure Boating to the West Wall | **A** | yes | scenario rules (bunkers, boats) |

| 8 Dash for the Sambre | **A** | yes | scenario rules, probably vehicles (§17) |
| 2 Advance on Chasoul | **B** | cards yes, **terrain/LOS no** | a Map B terrain + LOS database |
| 3 A Cold Morning in Belgium | **B** | cards yes, **terrain/LOS no** | Map B database |
| 4 D-Day: Night Drop | **B** | cards yes, **terrain/LOS no** | Map B database, night rules, paradrop entry |
| 5 Operation Pickpocket | **B** | cards yes, **terrain/LOS no** | Map B database |

Verified for this brief: all eight missions have mission-card, movement-reference, briefing and
victory data compiled (`assets/data/missions.json`, `mission_cards.json`,
`mission_cards_movement.json`); the rulebook's "Use Map A/B" line was read per mission section.
What does **not** exist is `terrain_map_b` / `los_map_b` — the fitted hex grid, terrain guesses and
line-of-sight database that Map A has. Map B's art is already in `assets/library/map/`.

Building the Map B database is the v1 pipeline run again (`tools/build_terrain_db.py` and the LOS
compiler, both in `../AMBUSH/`), plus the hand-correction pass Map A needed. Not research — but
not small, and it unlocks four missions at once.

**Also outstanding, and shared by several missions:** §17 Vehicles (cars and tanks, armour,
penetration — `penetrationrules.js` exists and is tested, the presentation and the German vehicle
cards do not), and each mission's own scenario-specific rules.

## 4. Three versions, with what each costs

**A. Squad persistence only** — the squad survives a mission, CP are awarded and spent, dead
soldiers are replaced, and the result is saved. With one mission implemented this means "replay
Mission 1 with a veteran squad". Cheap, self-contained, and immediately testable; it also makes the
existing game feel different, because a soldier you kept alive is measurably better next time.
Mostly presentation plus a save format.

**B. Persistence + the three Map A missions (6, 7, 8).** A real four-mission campaign on data that
already exists. The cost is each mission's scenario rules, which are unknown until each is read —
mission 8 probably needs vehicles.

**C. The full eight-mission campaign.** Adds the Map B database and four more missions, one of
them (4) with night rules and a paradrop entry. This is the whole rest of the game.

## 5. What I would recommend, and why

**A first, and stop there until it has been played.** The campaign's appeal in the rulebook's own
words is that "individual soldiers acquire a history" — that is testable with one mission and a
persisted squad, and it is the only part of §16 that is cheap. It also produces the deed-tracking
and squad-record work that B and C need anyway, so nothing is wasted.

Against building B or C next: v2's open question is still whether the *presentation* is fun
(tasks 3–6 are built but unplayed). Three more missions is a large bet placed before that answer.

## 6. Decisions I would need from Wan before building A

1. **CP distribution:** ask the player to allocate the discretionary CP (faithful, one more
   screen), or auto-allocate by the §16/2 table and let the player adjust (fewer presses, same
   numbers)? My recommendation: auto-allocate from the recorded deeds, editable — it keeps the
   "press for a decision" rule and still lets the player overrule.
2. **Where the campaign lives:** a separate campaign page that launches missions, or Mission 1's
   own page with a "continue campaign" entry on the start screen?
3. **Persistence:** `localStorage` (survives on one browser, invisible to the player) or an
   exported save file the player keeps (works across machines, matches the existing export/import
   save)? My recommendation: both, as the existing save system already does.
4. **Scope confirmation:** is A the next task at all, or does the presentation review (tasks 3–6)
   come first and the campaign wait?
